Guide to Establishing and Running a Thai Foundation

A complete walkthrough of the registration process, required documents, legal requirements, and annual audit and reporting duties — plus how to draft each document — compiled from the official forms and regulations used when filing with a foundation registrar in Thailand.

Based on the Civil and Commercial Code provisions on foundations (มูลนิธิ) and the related official forms (Mor.Nor. 1, Mor.Nor. 2, Mor.Nor. 6, Mor.Nor. 7, and the committee-member certification form). Use this as preparatory reference only — always confirm current requirements with the local registrar before filing.

From structuring the foundation before filing, through to annual reporting duties, changes of committee members or bylaws, and the steps to dissolve a foundation — laid out in chronological order so you can see the whole lifecycle at a glance.

A

Before Filing — Structuring the Foundation

Define the foundation's objectives

State the purpose clearly, avoid wording so broad that the registrar cannot evaluate it, and make sure it matches the activities you actually intend to carry out — e.g. education, patient welfare, religious support, or another public-benefit purpose.

Arrange the initial endowment

A foundation must have assets set aside for its purpose from the outset. The asset owner must give a written pledge to transfer the assets to the foundation once registration is complete.

Document: Letter of Pledge (deed of commitment)

Choose the foundation's name

The name should reflect the purpose and must not duplicate or closely resemble another organization's name in a way that could cause confusion. If a person's or organization's name is used as part of the foundation's name, written permission from the name's owner is required.

Document: Letter of Permission to Use a Name (if applicable)

Secure an office location

A clear address is required for both the head office and any branch offices, together with the site owner's or possessor's written consent and a simple location map.

Document: Letter of Permission to Use the Premises

Select the prospective committee members

Every prospective committee member must complete a self-certification form disclosing nationality, occupation, assets, any prior work with other charitable organizations, and any criminal or civil case history.

Document: Certification of a Prospective Committee Member

Draft the bylaws

Draft the bylaws to cover every category the law requires (see the "Legal Requirements" tab for details) — the bylaws are the long-term rulebook for how the organization is run.

B

Founding Meeting

Hold the founders' meeting

The meeting must resolve to adopt the bylaws, appoint the first governing committee, and authorize a representative to file the application with the registrar. Record every resolution in the minutes.

Document: Minutes of the Founding Meeting
C

Filing the Registration Application

File the application to establish the foundation

File with the foundation registrar for the relevant area (Bangkok: the district office; other provinces: the district or provincial office), together with a complete supporting document set — typically three copies of each item.

The registration officer reviews the documents

If inconsistencies are found — for example, the committee list in the bylaws not matching the meeting minutes, or an over-broad statement of purpose — the officer will request corrections before proceeding.

The registrar approves the application

Once the documents are complete and correct, the registrar issues the certificate of foundation registration, giving the foundation legal-entity status under the law.

D

After Registration — Ongoing Annual Duties

Set up accounting and tax registration

Apply for a taxpayer identification number, open a bank account in the foundation's name kept separate from any committee member's personal account, and have the foundation's official seal made.

Carry out activities and keep records

Keep evidence of donations, donation receipts, expense documents, and activity/project reports on an ongoing basis starting from year one.

Close the annual financial statements

Prepare the annual financial statements and have them reviewed by an auditor before presenting them to the committee (see the "Closing Accounts & Audit" tab for details).

File the annual activity report

Submit the annual report on the foundation's operations to the registrar every year, summarizing the committee, objectives, activities, finances, and assets.

Document: Annual Report on Foundation Operations
E

When Something Changes

Change committee members or amend the bylaws

Hold a committee meeting to pass the relevant resolution, record it in the minutes, then file an application to register the change with the registrar. Amending the bylaws also requires a comparison table showing the old and new wording side by side.

Document: Application to Register New/Changed Committee Members or Bylaw Amendments
F

Dissolving the Foundation (if applicable)

Resolve to dissolve the foundation

The meeting resolves to dissolve the foundation and appoints a liquidator, then notifies the registrar of the dissolution together with the supporting documents.

Document: Notice of Foundation Dissolution

The liquidator carries out the liquidation

Publish notice of the dissolution in a newspaper, notify creditors in writing, prepare a balance sheet as of the dissolution date, dispose of assets, and settle all debts — with an auditor reviewing the process.

Report the completed liquidation

Once liquidation is complete, the liquidator reports the results to the registrar. The foundation then ceases to exist as a legal entity.

Document: Liquidation Report

The key legal points relating to foundations — from the governing law and the registrar's authority through to what the bylaws and the committee members themselves are required to satisfy.

Governing Law and AuthorityBasics

1

The primary governing law

Foundations are governed by the provisions on foundations in the Civil and Commercial Code, together with the ministerial regulations issued under that Code, which set out the official forms, the registration procedure, and the various reporting duties.

2

The foundation registrar

The registration authority depends on where the foundation's head office is located.

Bangkok — the Bangkok Foundation Registrar (district office) Other provinces — the Provincial Foundation Registrar (district or provincial office)

What the Bylaws Must CoverBylaws

At minimum, the bylaws should cover the following categories so that the foundation can be governed in practice and pass registration review.

1

Name and emblem of the foundation

Together with the reason for or background to the name, where a person's or organization's name has been used as part of it.

2

Office location

Both the head office and any branch offices (if applicable).

3

Objectives

Stated clearly, without being broader than what the foundation will actually carry out.

4

Endowment and assets

The assets set aside for the foundation's purpose, and the criteria for spending them.

5

Committee qualifications, size, and term

Including the conditions for leaving office and for filling a vacancy.

6

Quorum and voting

The minimum number of committee members required to attend a meeting, and the majority needed to pass a resolution.

7

Annual general meeting and spending authority

The month the annual general meeting will be held, and the spending limit the chairperson or treasurer may approve without further authorization.

8

Amending the bylaws and dissolving the foundation

The method for requesting amendments, and the procedure to follow when winding up the foundation.

Committee Member Qualifications and DisclosuresCertification Form

1

Information a prospective committee member must disclose

  • Nationality — a foreign national must state their passport number, residence permit, and work permit (if any)
  • Occupation, position, income, and place of work
  • Assets currently held
  • Prior work history with other charitable organizations (if any)
  • Any criminal or civil case in which a court has issued a final judgment
2

Committee size and term (typical practice)

Most bylaws set the committee size at not fewer than 3 and not more than 15 members, with a term of 2–3 years, and generally prohibit serving the same position for more than one consecutive term, except for positions the bylaws specifically exempt. The actual figures must be set out in each foundation's own bylaws.

Practical Details Often OverlookedPractice

1

Authorizing someone else to file documents

Must be done through a written power of attorney bearing a 10-baht duty stamp, with a copy of the grantor's ID card attached.

2

A bylaw amendment only takes effect once registered

Any amendment must be filed for registration with the registrar before it takes effect, together with a comparison table showing the old wording against the new.

3

Standard for the officer/registrar ID card

The card size is 5.4 × 8.4 cm, the photo is 2.5 × 3 cm, and the official emblem sits within a circle 3.5 cm in diameter, stamped in red ink — useful reference for verifying the identity of an official you deal with.

A foundation has reporting duties to the registrar and must have its accounts audited every year — here is the sequence for closing the books, the documents to prepare, and the most common pitfalls.

Annual Reporting DutyTo the Registrar

Every year the foundation must file an annual operations report, which must include at least the following information.

  • Names and positions of the foundation's committee members
  • The foundation's objectives
  • Activities/projects carried out in pursuit of those objectives
  • Spending, broken down by activity and by office overhead
  • Income, broken down by source (interest, activities, donations, other income)
  • The foundation's assets at the start of registration and as of the report date
  • Committee members' tenure and terms of office
  • Any other matters requiring a report, or recommendations

Steps to Close the Annual Financial StatementsAccounting

Verify the organization's status and accounting period

Check the name, registration number, address, current committee list, bylaws, and bank account status all match reality before beginning the bookkeeping.

Gather income records

Break income down clearly, e.g. general donations, project-specific donations, sponsorships, and bank interest received.

Gather expense records

Every expense should have supporting documentation, such as receipts, payment vouchers, expense approvals, and proof of transfer.

Reconcile the bank accounts

Check the statement for every account against the recorded transactions, including accounts with little activity.

Post income and expenses to the accounts

Separate income, expenses, assets, liabilities, and cash on hand by category — do not lump different categories together.

Make year-end adjusting entries

For example, accrued interest receivable, accrued expenses payable, asset depreciation, and funds or donations earmarked for a specific purpose.

Prepare the annual financial statements

Show cash, bank deposits, assets, liabilities, income, expenses, and cash on hand in a way that traces back to the source documents.

Send the statements to an auditor

The auditor reviews the figures, supporting documents, bank transactions, and the reasonableness of significant entries.

Present the statements to the committee

Record the committee's acknowledgment or approval in the meeting minutes, in line with the authority the bylaws give it.

File the records by year

Keep separate files for income, expenses, bank records, tax, meetings, and financial statements so the following year's work is easier and past records can be checked if needed.

Checklist Before Sending Documents to the AuditorChecklist

  • Bank statements for every account
  • Prior-year financial statements
  • Prior-year auditor's report
  • Complete registration documents
  • An accurate current committee list
  • Donation receipts and donor list
  • Complete receipts or expense records
  • Project documents or activity reports
  • Withholding tax documents (if any)
  • Asset register and depreciation schedule
  • Minutes relevant to approving the financial statements
  • Any unexplained bank transfers (if so, an explanation is needed)

Points to Watch When Closing the BooksImportant

Donations need clear evidence — record who gave it, when, how much, and which account it entered. The total on donation receipts should reconcile with the amount received into the bank.
Expenses must relate to the foundation's objectives — a committee member's personal expenses, or any item without supporting documents, will be flagged by the auditor.
Statements must cover every account — including accounts with little activity, since they may still carry interest or a balance that needs to be recorded.
The committee must acknowledge and approve the statements — the financial statements are not solely the accountant's responsibility; the committee's role is central to the foundation's transparency.
Do not let several years' books go unclosed — if several years are outstanding, start with the earliest, since the opening balance from that year affects every year after it.

Liquidation Upon DissolutionIf Dissolving

Publish notice of the dissolution

Publish an announcement in a newspaper as the law requires.

Notify creditors in writing

So creditors can submit claims within the specified period.

Prepare the balance sheet as of the dissolution date

Together with a detailed statement of assets and liabilities, reviewed by an auditor.

Dispose of assets and settle debts

Pay creditors, collect from debtors, and record any case where a creditor cannot be located.

Report the liquidation results to the registrar

Attach all supporting documents to the report — once the registrar acknowledges it, the foundation ceases to exist as a legal entity.

Practical guidance for drafting each key document, with the outline each section should follow, to reduce the chance of being asked to revise it when you actually file.

1 Drafting the Foundation's Objectives

Write clearly and concretely, and make sure it matches the activities you will actually carry out. Avoid wording broad enough to be read several different ways.

  • Name the target group or issue specifically — e.g. orphaned children, low-income patients, students lacking funds for school
  • Use action-oriented language — e.g. promote, support, assist, preserve, conserve
  • If there are several objectives, list them as separate sub-items rather than one combined sentence
Comparison Too broad: "To help society in every way."
Clearer: "To provide scholarships to primary-school students who lack the financial means to continue their education."
2 Drafting the Bylaws

Organize the bylaws into categories in order so they are easy to review and cover everything the law requires (see the categories listed in the "Legal Requirements" tab).

  • Write each category as short, clear clauses — brevity is fine as long as the meaning is complete
  • State the committee size as a range, e.g. "not fewer than __ and not more than __ members," for flexibility
  • Set the committee term and the conditions for renewal clearly to avoid disputes later
  • State explicitly the spending limit the chairperson or treasurer may approve without further authorization
3 Drafting the Minutes of the Founding Meeting

Follow the standard seven-part structure.

  • State the meeting number and year (e.g. Meeting No. 1/2025)
  • State the date of the meeting
  • State the meeting location
  • List the name of every attendee
  • Record the three main resolutions: adopting the bylaws, authorizing the applicant, and appointing the first governing committee — together with the resolution reached on each
  • Have the secretary or an appointed member sign as the minute-taker
  • Have the chairperson, or whoever presided, sign as the reviewer of the minutes
Sample resolution wording "The meeting unanimously resolved to adopt these bylaws for submission to the authorities."
4 Drafting the Registration Application

State the applicant's details exactly as they appear on their ID card, and make sure the office location matches what is stated in the bylaws and the letter of permission to use the premises.

  • Write the foundation's name to match the bylaws exactly, letter for letter
  • State the head office and any branch office locations separately and clearly
  • Tick the attached-documents list to match exactly what is actually attached — neither more nor fewer items
5 Drafting the Letter of Pledge

State the person making the pledge and the assets or amount to be given clearly, along with the timing condition.

  • State the amount or assets both in figures and in words
  • State the timing condition for the transfer (typically within 30 days of successful registration)
  • Have the pledgor, the recipient, and two witnesses sign, and attach a bank certificate of the deposit account
6 Drafting the Letter of Permission to Use the Premises / Name

State the party granting permission consistently with the ownership evidence, and define the scope of the permission clearly.

  • For an individual: state their name and address, and attach proof of ownership or right of possession
  • For an agency or company: the signatory must be the most senior officer or someone specifically authorized to sign on the organization's behalf
  • State the start date of the permission and attach a location map
  • Always have the grantor and two witnesses sign
7 Drafting the Annual Operations Report

Follow the nine-part structure of the official form so it is easy to review and complete.

  • The foundation's committee members (with positions)
  • The foundation's objectives
  • Activities/projects carried out
  • The foundation's spending
  • The foundation's income (by category)
  • Sources of income
  • The foundation's assets (start and end of year)
  • Committee members' terms of office
  • Other matters requiring a report, or recommendations
This guide is compiled as introductory reference material only. The exact documents and procedures can vary by location and by the discretion of the individual registrar. Always confirm requirements with the relevant registration office before filing any actual documents.
